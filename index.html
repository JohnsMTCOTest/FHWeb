Disposition matrix

Make sure every pathway is considered

Compare options at a glance. When a provider says “we just do cremation,” you will still know what to ask.

Pathway Containers / Urns Participation Conflicts & Rules
Traditional Burial Wood/metal casket; many cemeteries require a vault or liner Pallbearers, procession, graveside lowering Vault and marker rules set by cemetery; embalming optional unless venue requires
Green/Natural Burial Untreated wood, wicker, or shroud; no vault Family lowering, soil placement, natural markers No embalming; cemetery must permit vault-free burial and biodegradable materials
Cremation Fiberboard/plywood container; urns in wood, metal, ceramic, biodegradable, art glass, vinyl Witness placement or start, urn display, scattering rituals Provider policies for witnessing; follow scattering rules for land/water
Alkaline Hydrolysis Biodegradable shroud or simple container; urn options mirror cremation Facility tours or viewing windows when offered Availability varies by state; confirm transport and witnessing policies
Natural Organic Reduction No container inside vessel; soil returned in biodegradable bags or urns Soil sharing ceremonies, planting rituals, donations to forests No embalming; timing depends on facility; confirm soil transport and donation rules
Memorialization

Personal touches that withstand scrutiny

Pair merchandise with experiences to match the person’s story and your chosen disposition.

Merchandise

Mushroom suit, tree pods, art glass, vinyl records, walking sticks for scattering, water-soluble urns.

Experiences

Eternal reefs, space flights, soil donations to forests, guided scattering hikes, or boat ceremonies.

Participation rules

Note who may travel with the remains, pour ashes into reef molds, or join on the vessel for sea burial.

Conflict checks

Embalming vs. green burial, depth/distance for sea burial, and vessel safety requirements for family guests.
