Burial

Traditional Burial vs. Green/Natural Burial

Both place the body in the earth; preparation and materials differ. Confirm cemetery rules first.

Traditional burial
  • Container: casket (wood, metal, or fiberboard); many cemeteries require an outer vault or liner.
  • Preparation: embalming optional unless a venue requires it; refrigeration is a valid alternative.
  • Service fit: funeral with viewing, graveside with honors, or paired memorial later.
  • Memorialization: headstone, marker, or upright monument per cemetery policy.
Green/Natural burial
  • Container: biodegradable shroud or simple untreated wood/wicker; no vaults.
  • Preparation: no embalming; cooling with refrigeration, ice, or dry ice.
  • Service fit: home funeral, graveside, or simple funeral without invasive preparation.
  • Memorialization: natural markers, GPS coordinates, or communal memorial boards.

Conflict check: embalming conflicts with green burial policy. Ask about vault and marker requirements before purchasing containers.

Cremation

Cremation pathway

Flame-based process with choices for containers, witnessing, urns, and final placement.

Containers & timing
  • Cremation container: fiberboard, plywood, or alternative container; select based on provider policy.
  • Witness options: ask whether family can view placement or start the process; schedule varies.
  • Timing: may follow a funeral or home vigil; refrigeration bridges any delay.
Urns & placement
  • Urn choices: wood, metal, ceramic, biodegradable paper/salt, art glass, vinyl record, or keepsake sizes.
  • Placement: keep at home, bury, place in a columbarium, scatter (land or water), eternal reef, or art integration.
  • Memorialization: engraving, photo plates, or incorporating remains into paintings or jewelry.
Alkaline hydrolysis

Water-based cremation

Uses water, heat, and alkaline solution. Resulting remains resemble cremation powder.

Process & containers
  • No flame used. A biodegradable shroud or simple container may be required by the provider.
  • Witnessing: some providers allow viewing windows or tours—ask early.
  • Preparation: no casket needed in the process; refrigeration before placement when required.
Urns & placement
  • Urn options mirror cremation: wood, metal, ceramic, biodegradable, art glass, or keepsake sizes.
  • Placement: burial, scattering, eternal reef, jewelry, or home display per local rules.
  • Memorialization: pair with a memorial service or graveside-style ceremony.
Natural organic reduction

Human composting

Enclosed, regulated process that turns the body into soil. Works well with participation rituals.

Process & compatibility
  • No container inside the vessel; biodegradable clothing or shroud is typical.
  • Cooling: refrigeration or ice prior to placement; embalming is not compatible.
  • Service fit: home vigil, memorial service, or soil placement ceremony afterward.
Soil & memorialization
  • Soil designation: portion kept by family (where allowed), donation to conservation land, or use in gardens.
  • Containers: biodegradable bags or urns for soil portions; tree pod planters for living memorials.
  • Memorialization: plantings, soil-sharing rituals, or markers at donation sites.
Conflict checks

Verify your selections work together

Prevent surprises by checking service ideas against disposition requirements and provider policies.

Common conflicts
  • Embalming conflicts with green burial and natural organic reduction.
  • Some cemeteries require vaults that conflict with green burial materials.
  • Sea burial requires approved coordinates, distances, depth, and biodegradable materials.
Personalization cues
  • Ask if family may accompany remains to the crematory, hydrolysis facility, or reef placement.
  • Confirm witnesses are allowed for cremation or hydrolysis before sending invitations.
  • Note accessibility and passenger limits for vessels if guests will travel for sea burial.
