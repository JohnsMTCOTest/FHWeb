Disposition

Compare Every Final Placement Option

The sections below outline burial (traditional and green), cremation, alkaline hydrolysis, and natural organic reduction (NOR). Each includes container choices, urn options, soil destinations, and notes about compatibility with services.

Decision reminder

“We just want a cremation” still involves choices: container material, witness or no, urn type, and whether the remains are kept, buried, scattered, placed in a reef, or turned into art glass or vinyl records. Use the lists to make sure nothing important is missed.

Burial

Traditional Burial and Green/Natural Burial

Both place the body in the earth; the materials and preparation differ. Confirm cemetery rules.

Traditional Burial
  • Container: casket (wood, metal, or fiberboard). Many cemeteries require an outer burial vault or liner.
  • Preparation: embalming optional unless required by venue; refrigeration is an alternative.
  • Service fit: funeral service with viewing, graveside, or memorial; can combine with military honors.
  • Memorialization: headstone, marker, or upright monument per cemetery policy.
Green/Natural Burial
  • Container: biodegradable shroud or simple untreated wood/wicker; no metal or concrete vaults.
  • Preparation: no embalming; refrigeration or dry ice used for cooling.
  • Service fit: home funeral, graveside, or simple funeral without invasive preparation.
  • Memorialization: natural markers, GPS coordinates, or communal memorial boards.

Conflict check: embalming conflicts with green burial policies. Ask cemeteries about vault and marker rules.

Cremation

Cremation Pathway

Applies to flame-based cremation with an on-site or partner crematory.

Containers & Witnessing
  • Cremation container: fiberboard, plywood, or alternative container; choose based on provider policy.
  • Witness options: family may view placement or start; schedule and capacity vary by provider.
  • Timing: can follow a funeral or home vigil; refrigeration may be needed if delayed.
Urns & Placement
  • Urn choices: wood, metal, ceramic, biodegradable paper/salt, art glass, vinyl record, or keepsake sizes.
  • Placement: keep at home, bury in a plot or columbarium, scatter (land or water), place in an eternal reef, or integrate into art.
  • Memorialization: engraving, photo plates, or incorporating remains into paintings or jewelry.
Alkaline Hydrolysis

Water-Based Cremation

Uses water, heat, and alkaline solution. Resulting remains are similar to cremation powder.

Process & Containers
  • No flame is used. A biodegradable shroud or simple container may be required by the provider.
  • No casket container is necessary during the process.
  • Families can often request witnessing windows or facility tours; ask in advance.
Urns & Placement
  • Urn options match cremation: wood, metal, ceramic, biodegradable, art glass, or keepsake sizes.
  • Placement: burial, scattering, eternal reef, or keepsake jewelry; check policies for water scattering.
  • Memorialization: same choices as cremation; pair with a memorial service or graveside gathering.
NOR

Natural Organic Reduction

Also known as human composting. Converts the body into soil through a controlled, enclosed process.

Process & Compatibility
  • No container needed inside the vessel; biodegradable clothing or shrouds are typical.
  • Embalming is not compatible. Cooling before placement uses refrigeration or ice.
  • Service fit: home vigil, memorial service, or graveside-style soil placement ceremony.
Soil & Memorialization
  • Soil designation: take a portion home (where allowed), donate to conservation land, or use in personal gardens.
  • Urn/containers: biodegradable bags or urns for soil portions; some families use planters or tree pods.
  • Memorialization: plantings, soil-sharing ceremonies, or commemorative markers at donation sites.
Conflict checks

Verify Choices Work Together

Before finalizing, confirm that your service preferences and disposition steps do not conflict with provider or environmental rules.

Common conflicts
  • Embalming is not allowed for green burial or natural organic reduction.
  • Some cemeteries require vaults that are incompatible with green burial materials.
  • Sea burial distances, depth, and biodegradable requirements must be met by the vessel operator.
Personalization & Participation
  • Families can often accompany remains to reef placements or sea burials when provider policies allow.
  • Ask early about witnessing cremation, hydrolysis, or NOR placement to reserve space.
  • Document who should speak, carry, lower, or pour soil/ashes during services.
