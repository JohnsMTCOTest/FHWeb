Gatherings

Choose how people will come together

Each format includes an at-a-glance purpose statement, timing notes, who can participate, and a compatibility cue for disposition choices.

Home funeral

Family-centered care at home

Care, vigil, and ceremony happen at home with support from friends, community members, or a consultant. Paperwork and cooling plans must be clear before the death occurs.

  • Best when rituals, privacy, or cultural traditions center the home.
  • Confirm who files the death certificate and transport permits.
  • Set roles for washing, dressing, hosting visitors, and coordinating transport.
Checklist
  • Cooling: refrigeration, dry ice, or cooling blankets for 24–72 hours.
  • Safety: clear pathways, privacy signage, and secure pets/children areas.
  • Next step: transport plan for burial, cremation, hydrolysis, or NOR.
  • Participation: decide who may assist with body care and when visitors are welcome.
Structured gathering

Funeral service

A ceremony with the body present. Works before burial, cremation, hydrolysis, or NOR if refrigeration is used instead of embalming.

  • Roles: officiant or celebrant, pallbearers, readers, musicians.
  • Spaces: house of worship, chapel, community hall, home, or outdoor venue (permits may apply).
  • Procession: on foot or vehicle; include accessibility and weather contingencies.

Personalization ideas

  • Story tables with photos, heirlooms, uniforms, recipes, or travel maps.
  • Guest contributions: write notes to place in a casket, shroud, urn, or NOR vessel.
  • Soundscape: live music, curated playlists, or recorded messages.
  • Processional details: motorcycles, bicycles, honor guards, or community groups.
Memorial service

Gather without the body present

Ideal after cremation, alkaline hydrolysis, or natural organic reduction, or when families need time to travel. Can follow burial or occur at a meaningful location.

  • Centerpiece options: urn, soil, reef mold, art glass, photo altar.
  • Hybrid attendance: livestream links, reserved microphones for remote speakers.
  • Memory capture: audio guest book, reflection cards, or collaborative playlist.
Support
  • Hospitality: signage, ushers, quiet room for children, and refreshments.
  • Schedule: allow time for travel and for remains to be ready if paired with NOR or hydrolysis.
  • Accessibility: parking, seating, captioning, and translation needs.
At the place of rest

Graveside service

A concise ceremony at the gravesite or interment location. Can stand alone or follow another service.

  • Elements: brief readings, military honors, soil or flower placement, lowering rituals.
  • Logistics: seating, shade, accessibility, and cemetery regulations (vaults, markers, shrouds).
  • Pairing: burial, urn burial, green burial, or placement of NOR soil.

Participation options

  • Invite guests to place flowers, soil, or biodegradable markers.
  • Allow family to help lower the casket, shrouded body, or urn when permitted.
  • Provide moment-by-moment prompts so participants feel guided and included.
Burial at sea

Committal on the water

A Coast Guard-compliant ceremony aboard a licensed vessel. Includes full-body burial in a weighted, biodegradable shroud/casket or scattering of cremated remains.

  • Requirements: distance-from-shore and depth rules; EPA reporting for some regions.
  • Containers: biodegradable shrouds, reef forms, water-soluble urns, or paper urns.
  • Combine with: land-based memorial service before or after the voyage.
Family experience
  • Travelers: confirm passenger limits, weather policies, and accessibility needs.
  • Participation: readings, flower scattering, or pouring ashes into reef molds.
  • Safety: motion sensitivity plans, life jackets, and respectful handling of remains on board.
