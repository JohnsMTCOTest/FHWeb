Service formats

Choose How Loved Ones Will Gather

Each service type below includes what it is, why people choose it, required steps, and personalization ideas. Use it to confirm you understand all options before selecting one or combining a few.

Home Funeral

Family or friends keep the body at home for care, vigil, or a small ceremony. States differ on filing death certificates and transportation; confirm local requirements for refrigeration, ice, or dry ice.

  • Best for: family-led rituals, cultural or faith traditions that center the home.
  • Plan for: permits, body care timeline (often 24–72 hours), and space for visitors.
  • Personalize: music, storytelling circles, memory tables, and meaningful readings.
Checklist
  • Confirm who will file the death certificate and transport permits.
  • Arrange cooling (refrigeration or ice packs) and privacy for body care.
  • List participants and roles for washing, dressing, or vigil hosting.
  • Decide on subsequent disposition (burial, cremation, hydrolysis, NOR).
Structured gathering

Funeral Service

A ceremony with the body present, often including visitation, viewing, or a procession. Suitable for traditional burial, green burial (without embalming), cremation, hydrolysis, or NOR when timed appropriately.

  • Roles: officiant or celebrant, pallbearers, readers, music leads.
  • Location: chapel, house of worship, community center, home, or outdoors (check permits).
  • Timing: may precede burial, cremation, or hydrolysis; refrigeration can replace embalming if desired.

Personalization ideas

  • Display story tables: photos, favorite items, awards, recipes, or travel mementos.
  • Invite guests to write notes for burial, cremation container, or NOR vessel.
  • Incorporate live music, recorded playlists, or a communal reading.
  • Offer a procession on foot, with motorcycles, bicycles, or other meaningful escorts.

Memorial Service

A service without the body present. Works well after cremation, alkaline hydrolysis, or natural organic reduction, or when remains are not available. It can be paired with burial or scattering later.

  • Best for flexible timing when family travels or a location is special to the person.
  • Can include an urn, soil, or memorial object such as art glass or a reef mold.
  • Use photo displays, audio recordings, or video tributes to tell the story.
Support
  • Coordinate speakers and invite remembrances in advance to ensure inclusive voices.
  • Plan hospitality: refreshments, quiet rooms, or live streaming for remote guests.
  • Gather keepsakes: programs, seed packets, or small memory cards for guests to take.
At the place of rest

Graveside Service

A concise ceremony at the grave or interment site. Often paired with a funeral or memorial service, or used alone for a simple yet meaningful farewell.

  • Elements: brief readings, military honors, soil or flower placement, or a lowering ritual.
  • Logistics: seating, shade, accessibility, and cemetery regulations (vault requirements, shrouds, markers).
  • Compatible with burial, urn burial, green burial, or interment of soil from natural organic reduction.

Participation options

  • Invite guests to place flowers, soil, or biodegradable markers on the grave.
  • Allow family to help lower the casket, shrouded body, or urn (if permitted).
  • Share a reflective walk to the gravesite, guided by music or narration.

Burial at Sea

A committal ceremony on a vessel that travels to an approved location. Options include full-body burial in a weighted, biodegradable shroud/casket or scattering of cremated remains.

  • Requirements: licensed captain, distance-from-shore and depth guidelines, and environmental compliance.
  • Containers: biodegradable shrouds, reef forms, paper urns, or water-soluble urns.
  • Paperwork: disposition permit and, when needed, EPA reporting (varies by region).
Family experience
  • Families can often ride along and participate by reading, scattering flowers, or pouring ashes into a reef mold.
  • Consider accessibility (motion sensitivity, seating) and weather contingencies.
  • Combine with a land-based memorial before or after the voyage.
