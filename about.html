About

Why This Resource Exists

The Personal Funeral Planning Guide was built so anyone—especially people new to the funeral profession—can confidently map out their own arrangements. It distills coursework topics into approachable explanations, checklists, and examples that answer the question: “Do I know all of my options before I decide?”

What you'll find
  • Service formats: home funeral, funeral service, memorial service, graveside, burial at sea.
  • Disposition routes: traditional burial, green/natural burial, cremation, alkaline hydrolysis, natural organic reduction.
  • Memorialization: merchandise, experiential tributes, and participation opportunities for families.
How to use it
  • Start on the service options page to decide the type of gathering.
  • Compare disposition paths on the disposition page to align with your values.
  • Choose personalization ideas on the memorialization page and record them in the checklists.
Values

Clarity, Accuracy, and Respect

The information emphasizes what is typically required (permits, timing, containers) and what may vary by jurisdiction. Always confirm local laws and provider policies, especially for home funerals, sea burial, alkaline hydrolysis, and natural organic reduction.

Conflicts to avoid

  • Embalming conflicts with green burial and natural organic reduction requirements.
  • Some cemeteries require vaults; green cemeteries typically prohibit them.
  • Sea burials have specific distance-from-shore and depth requirements; check carrier licenses.

Personalization reminders

  • Families may accompany remains to a reef deployment or sea burial when provider policies allow.
  • Memorial services can be held anywhere, including homes, parks, and community halls.
  • Witnessing cremation or hydrolysis is often possible with scheduling; ask providers early.
